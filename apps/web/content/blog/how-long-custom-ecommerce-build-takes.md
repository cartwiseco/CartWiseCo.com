### Timelines vary more for custom builds than for template-based stores

A Shopify store on a proven template can launch quickly. A custom build depends on scope, integrations, and how many rounds of revision the design goes through.

### Discovery and planning set the foundation

Before any design work starts, mapping out your catalog structure, user flows, and required integrations prevents costly rework later. Rushing this stage usually adds time elsewhere.

### Design typically takes longer than development expects it to

Custom visual design involves feedback rounds, and each round adds time. Having clear brand guidelines and reference examples ready before the project starts speeds this up significantly.

### Development and integration work in parallel

While the storefront is being built, backend work like payment gateways, shipping logic, and inventory systems can happen alongside it, which is one way experienced teams keep timelines realistic.

### Testing is not optional time

Checking checkout flows, payment processing, and mobile performance across devices takes real time, but skipping it is how stores end up fixing critical bugs after launch instead of before.

### A realistic range, not a fixed number

A straightforward custom store typically takes several weeks from kickoff to launch, while a more complex build with heavy integrations can take longer. Any partner giving you an exact date without understanding your scope first is guessing.

### Two different starting points

Shopify is built specifically for selling online. WordPress is a general content platform that becomes an e-commerce store through WooCommerce. That difference shapes almost everything else.

### Shopify: built for stores, not blogs

Shopify handles hosting, security, and checkout out of the box, which makes it a fast path to launch for small businesses that want to start selling without managing technical infrastructure.

### WordPress: better for content-led brands

If your business relies on blog content, SEO articles, or a content-heavy storefront to drive traffic, WordPress gives you more flexibility to combine content and commerce on one platform.

### Cost structures work differently

Shopify runs on a monthly subscription with transaction fees built into its ecosystem. WordPress requires separate hosting, and while the software itself is free, plugins and premium themes add up over time.

### Maintenance responsibility is the real trade-off

Shopify manages updates and security for you. WordPress gives you more control, but that control comes with more responsibility for keeping plugins, themes, and security patches current.

### Choose based on how you plan to grow

If speed to launch and low maintenance matter most, Shopify is usually the simpler path. If content marketing and deep customisation are core to your strategy, WordPress with WooCommerce is worth the extra setup.

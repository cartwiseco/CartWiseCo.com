### Launch-ready means more than products uploaded

A store can look complete with products and prices in place and still be missing the features that make customers trust it enough to check out.

### Clear product pages that answer real questions

Every product page should answer size, material, shipping time, and return policy without the customer having to search elsewhere. Missing details are one of the most common reasons for abandoned carts.

### A checkout that doesn't create friction

Guest checkout, multiple payment options, and a clear order summary reduce drop-off at the final step. This is often the highest-impact area to get right before launch.

### Working shipping and tax logic

Shipping costs and delivery estimates need to be accurate before launch, not adjusted after the first wave of customer complaints. Tax calculation should match your actual business requirements.

### Mobile performance, not just mobile design

A store that looks fine on mobile but loads slowly or has awkward tap targets will lose customers. Test the full purchase flow on an actual phone, not just a browser preview.

### Analytics and tracking set up from day one

Without conversion tracking in place at launch, you lose the ability to measure what's working in those critical early weeks. Set this up before traffic starts, not after.
